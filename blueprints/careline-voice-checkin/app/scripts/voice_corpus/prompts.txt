# CareLine self-voice corpus. One utterance per line; blank lines and #comments ignored.
# Read each line as you would actually SAY it on a warm, unhurried check-in call.
#
# Design notes:
#  - Section A is the real domain: things the agent actually says. Highest value,
#    because prosody transfer is register-specific and the existing 11.8h of
#    training audio is all lecture delivery, not gentle check-in delivery.
#  - Section B carries the emotional range the domain needs (concern, warmth,
#    reassurance) without which replies land flat.
#  - Section C is phonetic/prosodic coverage: questions, lists, numbers, dates,
#    names, and consonant clusters that rarely appear in section A.
#  - Lines are 2-9 seconds when spoken naturally, which fits the training cap.

# ---- A. greetings and openings ----
Hey, it's good to hear your voice today.
Hi, it's your check-in call. How are you doing?
Good morning. I just wanted to see how you're settling into the day.
Hello, it's me again. I've been thinking about you since we last talked.
Hi there. Is now still a good time to talk for a few minutes?
It's good to catch you. How has your morning been so far?
I wanted to check in and see how you're feeling today.

# ---- A. check-in questions ----

Have you managed to eat something this morning?
How's your energy been the last couple of days?
Did you get outside at all yesterday?
Has anyone stopped by to see you recently?
Are you keeping up with your medication alright?
How are you feeling in yourself today, honestly?
Is there anything that's been worrying you?
What's felt good this week, even if it was small?

# ---- A. memory and continuity ----
Last time we spoke you mentioned Emily's recital was coming up.
You told me on Tuesday that your knee had been bothering you.
When we talked on Saturday you said you'd been sleeping better.
You mentioned your son was going to call over the weekend.
I remember you said the garden was starting to come back.

# ---- A. acknowledging and reflecting ----

I'm glad you told me that.

I can hear that you're doing your best with it.
That's a lot to carry on your own.
It sounds like you've had more on your plate than usual.

# ---- A. gentle concern and escalation ----

That does sound like something we shouldn't leave alone.

Would it be alright if I passed that along to your nurse?

I'm not a doctor, but I don't think that should wait.

# ---- A. reassurance and closing ----

I'll let you get back to your afternoon.
You did the right thing telling me about this.
Rest if you can, and we'll talk again soon.

That's a real accomplishment, and I hope you're proud of it.

# ---- C. longer flowing sentences (prosodic arcs) ----
